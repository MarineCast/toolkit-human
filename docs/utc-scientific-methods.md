# UTC solar and civil-calendar research method

`land_reporting_opportunity.utc_context.integrate_land_context` defines an opt-in
research scenario. It does not replace native daily builders or establish measured
presence, reporting effort, detection probability, or production eligibility.
No regional products are generated by calling the test suite.

## Support and inputs

One call represents one explicitly declared location, source-to-target distance in
km, civil-calendar variant, and timezone-aware interval of at most 48 hours. The
reviewed domain is 2000–2035, latitude ±60 degrees, and the WA/BC civil zones
`America/Los_Angeles` and `America/Vancouver`. Calendar rows have unique naive
`local_date` labels and dimensionless `value` in [0,1] or null. The caller must
retain the jurisdiction and calendar field: the legacy cross-border holiday/season
proxy is not a jurisdiction-specific measure of behavior. Civil values describe
context applying throughout that civil day; they are not observed totals to prorate.

Weather rows have unique aware `valid_time_utc`, visibility in km, wind speed in
m/s, optional precipitation rate in mm/hour, and `sample_status` (`valid` or
`unavailable`). Retain source identity, checksums, acquisition/forecast issue time,
units, spatial support, and rights outside this computational helper. Do not infer
finer observed weather from a fine-resolution solar location. Interpolation using
future samples is a retrospective scenario, not an operational forecast.

## Definition

For interval length T, compute the joint integral of the requested factors divided
by T, rather than multiplying separately averaged factors. D(t) is the daylight
indicator and C(t) the civil-date context value. Exact civil-midnight overlaps
preserve 23/24/25-hour days. TZif data comes explicitly from the packaged `tzdata`
dependency; outputs record its package/IANA versions and zone-file SHA256.
Vancouver and Los Angeles must not be treated as aliases.

Solar coordinates use the continuous Julian-day approximation documented by
[USNO](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/sun_approx), combined with the solar-hour-angle
and zenith relation in [NOAA's equations](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/solareqns.PDF).
D(t)=1 when geometric solar-center altitude is at least −0.8333 degrees, the
[standard upper-limb sunrise convention](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/RST_defs).
Crossings are bracketed every 600 seconds and solved to 0.01 seconds. UTC
approximates UT1/TT; a level unobstructed horizon and fixed standard refraction are
assumed. This is astronomical daylight context, not observed illumination or a
terrain horizon calculation. Numerical root tolerance is not physical accuracy.

Interpolate each weather variable linearly between finite adjacent samples at
most six hours apart (configurable downwards); do not extrapolate or bridge a
null/unavailable sample. With visibility v, distance d, wind u, and sigmoid σ:

- V(t) = σ((v(t)−d)/max(1 km, 0.2 v(t))), with explicit V=0 for v=0.
- W(t) = σ((5.5 m/s−u(t))/1.5 m/s)^0.7.
- Calendar, DC, DV, DCV, and DCVW means are reported separately.

Parameters are configurable and recorded. These are inherited scenario response
shapes, not newly calibrated behavioral relationships. Static access/viewability
kernels are not applied here; any downstream spatial integration must preserve
source/target roles and avoid multiplying an already included distance weight twice.

For a midnight-to-midnight UTC day only, integrate precipitation rates to R in mm
and use P=(1+R/10 mm)^−0.3 in the DCVWP mean. R requires complete full-day rate
support. [NCEP PRATE](https://www.nco.ncep.noaa.gov/pmb/docs/grib2/grib2_doc/grib2_table4-2-0-1.shtml)
is a rate: convert kg/m²/s to mm/hour before supplying this API. Interpolated
instantaneous forecast rates yield an estimated accumulation, not an observed
accumulation. Never substitute a local-day precipitation total. Subintervals omit
the daily-rain-conditioned metric: rain mass is additive, but recomputing a nonlinear
daily response on arbitrary subintervals is not conservative.

## Missingness and numerical diagnostics

Known zero factors establish a zero product despite unknown other factors. Missing
calendar or weather is otherwise unknown. Joint metrics require support during
daylight; calendar alone requires support throughout the interval. Coverage is the
known fraction of that required duration. A partial value is the available
contribution divided by the full T, never normalized by available time. No known
required support yields null/unavailable. Lower/upper bounds add zero/one over
unknown required time; they are logical missing-support bounds conditional on this
model, not confidence intervals or source/model uncertainty estimates.

Integration splits at solar crossings, civil boundaries and weather sample knots.
Midpoint quadrature runs at the requested step and half that step; the reported
value uses the finer step. Their difference is a convergence diagnostic, not a
rigorous error bound. Output includes method version, support, parameters, statuses,
coverage, bounds and timezone provenance.

## Validation and qualification

```bash
python -m pytest -q tests/human/activity_and_effort/test_land_utc_context.py
```

Offline tests compare 12 sunrise/set events across six Seattle dates with retained
[USNO API](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/data/api) reference values (90-second tolerance),
and cover leap day, DST, year boundaries, WA/BC zone divergence, conservation,
nonlinear ordering, numerical refinement, null/zero/partial semantics and input
immutability. Fixture hashes identify original API responses, not the compact
extracted fixture bytes. CI repeats these tests from an installed wheel outside
the checkout.

This draft does not qualify a regional UTC land product. Retained-data spatial
pilots, resolution/parameter sensitivity, calibration and independent scientific
review remain open. Native local-day companions remain native. No existing
immutable release or application contract is modified.

AIS-dependent outputs remain withheld: inspected legacy provenance did not establish
an original provider, acquisition receipt, applicable terms, or checksum-linked
preprocessing chain. A public NOAA collection is a candidate new source, not
retroactive rights evidence for the legacy archive. Resolve original rights or
qualify a separately acquired source before using those outputs; Canadian coverage
and source eligibility require separate evidence.
